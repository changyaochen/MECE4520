Likelihood playground

Use this simulation to distinguish a probability model from the likelihood of observed data. A simulated box produces red tickets (coded as \(1\)) and blue tickets (coded as \(0\)). You control the probability used to generate the tickets, but the likelihood chart asks a different question: for the tickets we have already observed, which candidate value of \(p\) explains them best?

TipWhat to watch

At first, no candidate value is preferred. Generate a few tickets, inspect the likelihood curve, and try to identify its peak before revealing the MLE. As more tickets accumulate, the curve generally becomes more concentrated.

No tickets drawn yet.

For a fixed number of draws \(n\) and \(r\) observed red tickets, the likelihood is

\[ L(p) = \binom{n}{r}p^r(1-p)^{n-r}. \]

The plotted vertical scale is the likelihood itself. It is not a probability distribution over \(p\); it shows how well each candidate accounts for the observed tickets.
