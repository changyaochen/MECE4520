Explore AR, ARX, and recurrent models

The Silverbox benchmark is a measured electronic system designed to behave like a Duffing oscillator: a system with dynamics and a nonlinear restoring effect. It has one applied input, V1, and one measured response, V2. For a mechanical analogy, think of V1 as a force-like excitation and V2 as a displacement-like response.

Because V1 is deliberately applied to the physical system, its relation to V2 is causal rather than a generic feature–target association:

Applied causal input V1 = ut
drives→
Silverbox nonlinear dynamics
produces→
Measured response V2 = yt

Our task is to predict the next response from information available at that time. The models below make different assumptions about how the recent input and response determine that prediction.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{AR:}\qquad \widehat{y}_t &= \beta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j y_{t-j}, \\ \text{ARX:}\qquad \widehat{y}_t &= \beta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j y_{t-j} + \sum_{j=0}^{p}\gamma_j u_{t-j}, \\ \text{Response-only RNN:}\qquad h_t &= \tanh\!\left(W_h h_{t-1} + W_y y_{t-1} + b_h\right), \qquad \widehat{y}_t = W_o h_t + b_o, \\ \text{Input-aware RNN:}\qquad h_t &= \tanh\!\left(W_h h_{t-1} + W_y y_{t-1} + W_u u_t + b_h\right), \qquad \widehat{y}_t = W_o h_t + b_o. \end{aligned} \]

All four models are fitted on the first 25,000 training samples, then begin the held-out forecast with the last 20 responses of the complete training record. The intervening training samples are not used for fitting or warmup. AR and ARX retain those values explicitly as lag history. The two RNNs read them in time order to prime learned hidden states, then carry those states forward. Only the input-aware RNN receives the current known input alongside its previous response forecast.

TipWhat to watch

AR and the response-only RNN cannot react directly to a changing excitation because neither sees V1; moving from AR to a response-only RNN changes the representation of history, not the measured information. ARX and the input-aware RNN can use the applied input. ARX uses a fixed list of lags in a linear rule, whereas the input-aware RNN compresses earlier information into a nonlinear state and carries that state from one forecast step to the next. During training, backpropagation is truncated to manageable chunks, so this does not mean that its gradients extend indefinitely into the past.
