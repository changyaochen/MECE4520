How a decision tree grows

This deliberately small two-feature example shows how a decision tree builds a piecewise decision boundary. Red triangles are positive observations (label 1) and green circles are negative observations (label 0). The animation follows a single greedy tree: each new rule is applied only to the observations that reached its parent node.

TipWhat to notice

The vertical rules do not extend over the entire feature space. They apply only inside the upper or lower region selected by an earlier rule on (x_2). A tree therefore creates a sequence of conditional decisions—not one global equation.

Start with the labeled observations. No decision rule has been added yet.

At the end, the tree has seven leaves. The lower central leaf has a negative majority, even though it contains one positive observation. A leaf predicts its majority class; it does not need to be perfectly pure.
